# Interoperability, privacy, accessibility, security and India DPI requirements

**As-of date: 27 November 2026.** This report translates the named standards, laws, regulatory programmes and Indian education platforms into product requirements for an open-source school, college and university management platform. “Must support” means either a legal/customer procurement obligation in the relevant market or a practical integration requirement. A standard is not itself a law: conformance, certification, contract terms and local education-sector rules still have to be checked for each deployment.

## How to design for the whole market

The platform should separate a canonical education data model from adapters and policy controls. The canonical model should cover people, organisations, courses, classes, enrolments, attendance, assessments, results, credentials, competencies, staff, facilities, fees and audit events. Every field needs provenance, effective dates, source-system identifiers, sensitivity classification, retention rule and a data-sharing purpose. Adapters should expose REST/JSON, CSV and event interfaces, preserve external identifiers, support idempotent upserts, and keep an append-only interoperability/audit ledger.

The product should have tenant- and jurisdiction-level policy packs rather than one global default. A US K–12 tenant needs FERPA/COPPA/PPRA and state-contract controls; an EU or UK tenant needs GDPR-style rights, international-transfer and child-design controls; an Indian tenant needs DPDP controls plus UDISE+, APAAR, DigiLocker/NAD, ABC, DIKSHA and regulator reporting. Accessibility, security, consent, retention, breach and export functions must be built into the core rather than bolted on to each connector.

A useful release and procurement rule is: implement the base specification, publish versioned API documentation and test fixtures, then obtain a certification where a market or buyer values it. 1EdTech says its standards are free to download and use, but claiming conformance requires membership and completion of the relevant certification process. [1]

## Privacy and child-data law

### US FERPA, COPPA, PPRA and state student-privacy laws

**FERPA.** FERPA is implemented in 34 CFR Part 99 and applies to educational agencies and institutions receiving covered US Department of Education funds. The regulation defines education records as records directly related to a student and maintained by the institution or a party acting for it; it gives parents inspection, amendment and consent rights, transferring to the student at age 18 or attendance at postsecondary education. [13] The “school official” and legitimate educational interest routes do not make a vendor the owner of the data; institutions remain responsible for contract, access, disclosure, redisclosure and recordkeeping decisions.

Product requirements are a role- and purpose-based permission model; parent/eligible-student access and amendment workflows; annual-notice templates and multilingual/accessible delivery; disclosure/redisclosure ledger; export and hold controls; directory-information opt-out; school-official contract metadata; vendor subprocessor register; retention/deletion and legal-hold policies; encryption; and audit logs that can be produced to the institution. The platform should distinguish a minor’s parent from an eligible postsecondary student and support custody/authorisation evidence.

**COPPA, including the 2025 rule.** COPPA applies to operators of child-directed websites/services and operators with actual knowledge that they collect personal information online from children under 13. The FTC rule requires notice, verifiable parental consent, parental review/deletion/control, reasonable security, retention/deletion controls and no conditioning participation on more information than reasonably necessary. [14] The FTC’s April 22, 2025 final rule expanded/clarified definitions and obligations, including mobile telephone, government identifier and biometric identifiers, information security, retention/deletion, notices and consent methods. It expressly did **not** finalise proposed ed-tech school-authorisation provisions while DOE FERPA amendments were pending. [15]

Time-sensitive status: the 2025 amended rule became effective on **23 June 2025**. Regulated entities generally had until **22 April 2026** to comply, except specified provisions in 16 CFR 312.11(d)(1), (d)(4) and (g). [15] As of 27 November 2026, this is an operative rule, not a proposed amendment. Build neutral age-screening for mixed-audience surfaces; parent verification and consent records; child-directed privacy notices; parent review/delete/stop-collection controls; no targeted advertising or unnecessary profiling in school use; retention schedules; security incident evidence; processor/subprocessor controls; and a data inventory that treats biometrics and device/contact data as sensitive.

**PPRA.** The Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment gives parents rights concerning certain surveys, inspections and marketing involving students, including inspection of instructional material and policies for covered surveys and collection/use of personal information for marketing. The US Department of Education administers FERPA and PPRA through its Student Privacy Policy Office. [16] Platform implications are configurable survey question-classification and parental-notice/consent workflows, material-access logs, marketing/third-party sharing blocks, district policy publication, and exportable evidence of which students/parents were notified or opted out.

**State laws, including California SOPIPA.** US states add contract, purpose, security, deletion, sale/advertising, profiling and data-breach rules. California’s Student Online Personal Information Protection Act is a baseline example for operators of online services used primarily for K–12 school purposes. [17] A worldwide product should not rely on FERPA alone: maintain a state-law rule engine, prohibit sale/targeted advertising on education data, enforce no secondary commercial use, support district-controlled deletion/return at contract end, subprocessor approval, data security and student/parent rights. Maintain per-state data maps because the population covered, age thresholds, notice, breach and retention requirements differ.

### EU GDPR, UK GDPR and the UK Age Appropriate Design Code

**EU GDPR.** GDPR is a directly applicable EU regulation for processing personal data in the EU/EEA and for non-EU organisations offering goods/services to or monitoring people there. Its principles include lawfulness/fairness/transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality and accountability; it also provides data-subject rights, controller/processor obligations, breach and impact-assessment duties, special-category safeguards and international-transfer rules. The GDPR text is the primary source. [18] Child consent for information-society services is subject to Article 8’s age and Member State variation, so a platform must not hard-code a single global age or assume a school contract resolves every child-data issue.

Use a records-of-processing and data-inventory module; configurable lawful-basis and special-category flags; DPIA templates; controller/processor and subprocessor contracts; data-subject access, correction, restriction, portability and erasure workflows; privacy-by-default settings; breach triage and notification timers; DPO/contact routing; data residency and transfer mechanisms; encryption/key management; minimisation and retention automation; and human review of automated decision/profiling. Separate institutional controller configuration from a vendor processor role.

**UK GDPR and DPA 2018.** The UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 provide the UK framework. ICO guidance covers controllers/processors, lawful basis, rights, security, accountability, international transfers and children’s information. [19] A UK deployment therefore needs the same core controls as EU GDPR but should support UK-specific documentation, regulator contacts, transfer risk assessments/IDTA or applicable mechanism, and UK education-sector retention and safeguarding policies.

**UK Age Appropriate Design Code.** The ICO Code is a statutory code of practice for online services likely to be accessed by children; it operationalises the UK GDPR in product design. It sets 15 flexible standards: best interests; DPIA; age-appropriate application; transparency; no detrimental use; policies/community standards; high-privacy defaults; minimisation; sharing; geolocation; parental controls; profiling; nudge techniques; connected devices; and online tools. [20] The product should default child accounts to high privacy, collect the minimum, switch geolocation off, avoid manipulative prompts and child profiling, give age-appropriate notices, expose parental controls without taking away the child’s rights, keep data-sharing purpose-limited, and retain a design/DPIA evidence trail.

### UK DfE school census and statutory returns

England’s DfE school census guidance is a customer-facing statutory return specification. For 2026–27 it was updated **13 October 2026**, covers autumn 2026 and spring/summer 2027, and states that MIS systems contain the required items even though names and implementation differ. [21] Items include school identifiers and governance, pupils, classes, teachers and support staff, admissions appeals, attendance-related and special-educational-needs data, free-school-meal data, childcare, school time and other modules with collection timing and codes.

A UK MIS connector needs versioned census schemas, code-list validation, collection-period rules, school-phase/governance applicability, pupil/class reconciliation, exception and error messages, audit of source values and changes, XML/CSV or the then-current submission route, and a “generate return” preview with sign-off. Do not treat the DfE census as a generic UK schema: it is England-specific and must be maintained each academic year.

### Australia Privacy Act

Australia’s Privacy Act 1988 regulates Australian Government agencies and organisations above the relevant turnover threshold, with exceptions and additional covered entities. It contains 13 Australian Privacy Principles governing collection, notice, use/disclosure, quality, security, access/correction and cross-border handling. [22] An education platform should support APP privacy notices, purpose limitation, access/correction, secure deletion, cross-border disclosure and supplier controls, data-quality workflows, privacy impact assessments, incident response and the Notifiable Data Breaches process.

Time-sensitive child-data note: the 2024 Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act mandated an OAIC Children’s Online Privacy Code. OAIC’s page, updated **5 August 2026**, says the Code applies to covered online services likely to be accessed by children or primarily concerned with children’s activities and must be finalised/registered by **10 December 2026**. [23] Treat this as an active implementation deadline on the as-of date, not as a completed final code; design for high privacy, age-appropriate transparency, minimisation, child-centred risk assessment and no unnecessary profiling while monitoring the final instrument.

## India: privacy, policy and education DPI

### Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Rules 2025

The DPDP Act defines a **child** as an individual who has not completed 18 years, applies to processing in India and to offshore processing connected with offering goods/services to people in India, and makes a Data Fiduciary responsible for processing done by it or its Data Processor. The Act requires lawful purpose, notice, consent or certain legitimate uses, purpose-limited data, reasonable security safeguards, breach intimation, erasure when the purpose is no longer served unless law requires retention, contact information and grievance redressal. [24]

For children, the Act requires verifiable parental consent before processing child personal data and prohibits tracking or behavioural monitoring of children and targeted advertising directed at children, subject to statutory exceptions. It also gives the Central Government power to notify Significant Data Fiduciaries with additional duties. Build age/child-status and guardian relationship records, parent-verification evidence, consent notices in English or an Eighth Schedule language, withdrawal propagation to processors, a consent ledger, purpose/field minimisation, child-safe defaults, no behavioural advertising, rights requests, grievance handling and deletion/retention orchestration.

The government notified the DPDP Rules 2025 on **14 November 2025**. The official phased commencement is important: Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 commenced on publication; Rule 4 commenced one year later; Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 commence 18 months after publication. [25] Therefore, as of 27 November 2026, Rule 4 is in force and the detailed child-consent, security, breach, retention and rights mechanisms in Rules 3 and 5–16 are scheduled for the 18-month date in May 2027, unless a later notification changes that position. A product may implement them early, but must not mislabel future provisions as already operative.

Rule 10 specifies verifiable parental consent and reasonable diligence to establish that the consenting person is an identifiable adult, using reliable identity/age information or voluntarily provided information/token from an authorised entity, including a Digital Locker service provider. [25] Build an abstraction layer so verification can use institution-held adult evidence, an authorised identity/age token or DigiLocker-linked flow without unnecessarily storing the parent’s identity document. Rule 6 of the notified Rules describes encryption, masking/tokenisation, access control, logs/monitoring/review, backup/continuity, processor contract safeguards and organisational measures; Rule 7 requires prompt individual notice and Board reporting, with a 72-hour update/detail window stated in the text. [25]

The Act’s maximum penalty for failure to take reasonable security safeguards is up to **₹250 crore**; breach-notification and child-obligation contraventions can reach **₹200 crore**, and other listed contraventions can reach **₹50 crore**. These are statutory maximums, not automatic fines. [26] Build an evidence pack: records of processing, consent and notices, processor contracts, access/log reviews, security testing, backup/restore tests, incident timeline, Board/individual notices, rights tickets and deletion proofs.

### NEP 2020 and the architecture direction

NEP 2020 is the policy context for competency-based, multidisciplinary, flexible and technology-enabled education. The operational product consequence is not one NEP API. It is a need for stage/grade flexibility, multilingual and inclusive content, competency/outcome tracking, formative and holistic assessment, vocational and higher-education pathways, credit mobility, teacher development and data-informed improvement. DIKSHA’s current portal reflects NEP stage structure from Foundational through Secondary and supports board/state tenants, textbooks, video, interactive content, courses and accessibility settings. [27]

### UDISE+

UDISE+ is India’s Ministry of Education school-education data platform. Its current portal provides school search, dashboards, archives, microdata requests, GIS, PGI, a data-sharing portal, a developer portal, data-capture formats and 2025–26 reports; the notice board shows AY 2026–27 data completion. [28] The product needs a UDISE+ reporting adapter with UDISE codes, school/teacher/student/class/programme/facility and outcome data, versioned data-capture formats, validation, role-based state/district/school views, submission status and reconciliation. Keep UDISE reporting separate from the operational canonical record so corrections and source ownership are explicit.

### APAAR ID, Academic Bank of Credits, DigiLocker and NAD

APAAR is the “One Nation, One Student ID” Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry. Its official portal says parents submit voluntary consent for minors; UDISE generates APAAR against the student’s PEN; the ID is pushed into DigiLocker; and the 12-digit identifier links academic credits, marksheets, degrees, diplomas, certificates and co-curricular accomplishments. [29] The platform must treat APAAR as an external identifier, not as a replacement for local IDs: store consent and provenance, support duplicate/mismatch resolution, never expose it unnecessarily, and make joins reversible and auditable.

ABC is the Ministry of Education’s Academic Bank of Credits, regulated by UGC and developed/maintained by Digital India Corporation. The official portal describes secure storage, accumulation, transfer and redemption of credits from recognised institutions, integrated with APAAR, DigiLocker and NAD, and provides an API directory. [30] Implement course/credit structures, academic terms, outcomes, transfer/redemption status, awarding-institution authority, APAAR linkage, signed record packages, learner consent, DigiLocker/NAD delivery and correction/revocation workflows. The record model should handle formal, vocational and recognised non-academic learning.

### DIKSHA

DIKSHA is a national school-education digital platform linked from the Ministry/NCERT ecosystem. The portal supports NCERT, CBSE, NIOS and state/UT tenants; NEP stages; multilingual resources; QR-linked textbooks; courses, virtual labs and teacher/student content; and accessibility options such as text-to-speech, text size, line-height, link highlighting, dyslexia-friendly display and hide-images mode. [27] Integrate through documented APIs/tenant contracts rather than scraping. Support content/resource IDs, board, medium, grade, subject, language, licensing, accessibility metadata, progress/credential exchange and offline/mobile constraints.

### PARAKH Holistic Progress Card

PARAKH/NCERT’s Holistic Progress Cards implement the NEP shift from rote knowledge acquisition to competency-based and holistic assessment. The HPC covers Foundational, Preparatory, Middle and Secondary stages and captures performance across curricular elements and multidisciplinary activities. [31] A platform should model competency evidence, teacher/self/peer/parent feedback where used, activities, rubrics, developmental narratives, stage transitions, languages and generated cards. Support a digital HPC export/import and preserve the underlying evidence rather than only a rendered PDF.

### CBSE and state-board requirements

Boards are not one national API. CBSE, NIOS and each state board can differ in registration, candidate data, subject/combination codes, examination forms, internal assessment, attendance, practicals, results, migration/transfer certificates, correction windows, language and submission calendars. DIKSHA’s explicit board/state tenant structure illustrates this variation. [27] Use a board-adapter registry, versioned code lists, bulk file/API exchange, field-level validation, approval and correction workflow, digital-signature/evidence storage, and configurable retention. Never hard-code CBSE fields as universal Indian education fields.

### NAAC, binary accreditation and AQAR

NAAC is an autonomous UGC institution for assessment and accreditation of higher-education institutions. Its current site exposes HEI/assessor portals, assessment/accreditation processes, criteria and weightages, AQAR and IQAC workflows. [32] NAAC’s reform notice records a move toward **Binary Accreditation** followed by Maturity Based Graded Levels; it also states transition rules for institutions with pending or valid RAF applications. [32] As of November 2026, implement both legacy RAF-grade evidence and the new binary/maturity evidence model, with a configurable versioned self-study repository.

Product features include criterion/metric evidence, programme and institution scope, IQAC actions, AQAR cycles, document/version/approver workflow, faculty/publication/infrastructure/student-outcome data, survey evidence, data lineage, assessor access and immutable submission packs. Keep accreditation evidence distinct from operational analytics and provide institution-controlled export.

### NBA

The National Board of Accreditation accredits programmes, especially engineering and other professional programmes, using an online Accreditation Workflow Management System; its site provides accreditation, application and accredited-programme workflows and a new Self-Assessment Report for UG Engineering Tier I. [33] Build programme-level outcome-based education entities: programme educational objectives, graduate attributes, course outcomes, CO–PO/PSO mappings, attainment calculations, assessment evidence, faculty, facilities, continuous-improvement actions and SAR export. Support tier/discipline/version-specific templates and evidence locks.

### NIRF

NIRF is the Ministry of Education’s National Institutional Ranking Framework, launched in 2015. Its parameters include Teaching, Learning and Resources; Research and Professional Practice; Graduation Outcomes; Outreach and Inclusivity; and Perception. [34] A university platform should maintain the denominator definitions, academic-year snapshots and evidence for faculty, students, research, sponsored projects, patents/publications, placement/graduation outcomes, finance, diversity and outreach. Provide a reviewable NIRF data mart and data dictionary rather than a one-click “ranking score” with no lineage.

### AISHE

AISHE is an annual web-based Ministry of Education survey covering higher-education institutions and collecting teachers, enrolment, programmes, examination results, finance and infrastructure. The portal states that the 2025–26 survey is open and points to institution registration, web DCF and 2023–24 reports. [35] Implement AISHE code and institution master, year-specific DCF schemas, programme/teacher/student/finance/infrastructure fields, validation and approval, submission status and export. Treat AISHE as a reporting snapshot, not the authoritative live student record.

### Samarth eGov university ERP

Samarth is an Indian higher-education digital-campus/ERP ecosystem used for admissions, student and faculty workflows, examinations, internal assessment, administration and related university services. Its official site provides capability guides for admin, faculty and students and records deployments for admissions and data management across universities/colleges. [36] For interoperability, expose modular APIs and import/export for institution, programme, applicant, admission, fee, course, registration, attendance, internal/practical marks, examination, results, transcripts, faculty/staff, finance and reporting. Support university/state tenancy, workflow configuration, Indian languages, Aadhaar/APAAR/DigiLocker/ABC as optional external services, and clean migration from existing Samarth deployments.

## Security and assurance

**SOC 2.** SOC 2 is an AICPA CPA-attestation service/report over controls relevant to Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and/or Privacy; it is not a product security certification or a law. AICPA describes SOC as a suite of assurance reports used by users to assess outsourcing risk. [41] A commercial SaaS platform should maintain a control matrix, system description, risk assessment, logical/physical access, change management, vulnerability/incident response, backup/DR, vendor management, privacy and evidence collection suitable for a Type 2 report. Make the report scope, period, exceptions and subservice organisations explicit.

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022.** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 specifies requirements for an information security management system. It is an international, certifiable management-system standard covering risk management, confidentiality, integrity and availability; certification is optional but widely used in procurement. [42] Build an ISMS around asset/data inventory, risk register, statement of applicability, policies, access reviews, secure development, supplier/cloud controls, incident management, business continuity, internal audit, management review and corrective actions. Align the open-source project’s contribution and release process with secure SDLC, dependency governance and vulnerability disclosure.

**OWASP ASVS 5.0.** ASVS is an open application-security verification standard for designing, developing and testing web applications and services. Version **5.0.0 was published 30 May 2025**; its requirements provide a metric, guidance and procurement baseline. [43] Use versioned ASVS IDs in acceptance criteria. Cover architecture, encoding/injection prevention, validation, authentication, session management, access control, cryptography, error handling/logging, data protection, communications, malicious code, business logic, files, configuration and API security. Add automated SAST/DAST/dependency/container scans, threat modelling, penetration testing, fuzzing, secrets detection, SBOM/provenance and reproducible builds.

## Implementation priorities

1. **Core trust layer:** canonical model, external IDs, purpose/consent/guardian records, data classification, retention/deletion, rights/grievance workflows, encryption, tenant isolation, audit ledger, backups and incident response.
2. **Global interoperability:** OneRoster CSV/REST, LTI 1.3 Advantage, QTI, Caliper, CASE, Open Badges/CLR, xAPI/cmi5; then Ed-Fi/CEDS/SIF adapters where target buyers require them.
3. **India pack:** UDISE+ schemas and API/data-capture connector; APAAR/PEN/DigiLocker consent and mismatch handling; ABC/NAD credit and award exchange; DIKSHA content/tenant integration; PARAKH HPC; board-adapter registry; AISHE/NIRF/NAAC/NBA evidence and reports; Samarth migration/API pack.
4. **Accessibility and assurance:** WCAG 2.2 AA design system, EN 301 549/Section 508/GIGW mappings, accessible document generation, ASVS-based secure SDLC, ISO 27001 ISMS and SOC 2 evidence readiness.
5. **Jurisdiction operations:** release calendars and schemas for DfE census, US state laws and contracts, EU/UK transfer and child-code rules, Australian APP/NDB and Children’s Online Privacy Code, and India’s DPDP commencement/Rules updates. Date every legal/configuration pack and expose a customer-facing change log.

The strategic conclusion is that worldwide saleability is less about supporting every acronym in one database than about providing a stable canonical record, versioned conformance adapters, explicit consent and provenance, accessible interfaces, and evidence that a school or university can export, correct, delete, audit and govern its data without vendor lock-in.
